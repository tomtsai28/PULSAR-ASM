Abstract / 執行摘要

English Abstract — A prevalent conceptual hypothesis in recent neural network compression asserts that large language model (LLM) representations mirror continuous sensory signals (such as audio MP3 or JPEG imagery), exhibiting dominant energy concentration in low-frequency spectral bands that can be pruned via 1D Discrete Fourier Transforms (DFT). In this technical note, we present definitive empirical and theoretical falsification of this hypothesis across an 18-layer bare-metal Google Gemma-2B architecture. By executing Graph Laplacian spectral permutation via Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the Fiedler vector ($\pi = \text{argsort}(v_2)$), we show that while early token embedding representations (Layer 00) demonstrate pronounced semantic clustering—surging low-frequency energy ($k \le 64$) from 5.73% to 15.36% (a 2.68× amplification)—all subsequent hidden representations (Layers 01 to 17) exhibit an insurmountable Topological Dimension Barrier. Deep residual representations converge to an equiangular white-noise spectrum (mean spectral flatness $\gamma \approx 0.58$, spectral centroid locked at $k \approx 512 / 1024$), restricting low-frequency power to 6.2% ~ 7.7%. We prove via differential topology that a 2048-dimensional non-Euclidean hyperspherical manifold cannot be mapped onto a 1D linear array without boundary tearing and metric collapse, causing 1D low-pass filtering to catastrophically corrupt vocabulary-level argmax discrimination. Finally, we demonstrate that high-dimensional orthogonal subspace steering (Null-Space projection and carrier injection) is the sole mathematically sound mechanism for non-destructive intervention and bitwise $L2=0$ cognitive rollback.

1. Introduction: The 1D-FFT Analogy Fallacy

The application of classical harmonic analysis to deep learning has recently sparked considerable interest, spanning unparameterized token mixers (FNet) to frequency-domain KV-cache truncation (FreqKV, FlashCache). A seductive intuitive corollary posits that because artificial neural networks are trained with gradient descent on natural data, their latent representations must exhibit low-frequency spectral bias, allowing model compression via simple 1D low-pass Fourier or Cosine filtering:

x_{\text{pruned}} = \mathcal{F}^{-1} \Big( \text{rect}_{K_{\text{cutoff}}}\big( \mathcal{F}(x_{\text{residual}}) \big) \Big)

While this paradigm holds rigorously for continuous media (where biological perception possesses smooth low-pass spatial/temporal tolerance), it introduces a fatal mathematical fallacy when applied to transformer residual streams:

  1. Continuous Perceptual Tolerance vs. Discrete Extreme Discrimination: In audio or imagery, discarding 10% of high-frequency energy induces mild blurring or acoustic softening without semantic collapse. In autoregressive language models, final token emission is dictated by an extreme 256,000-way categorical competition ($\text{Argmax} / \text{Softmax}$). Subtle orthogonal features in the residual stream often separate semantically similar tokens (e.g., distinguishing entity IDs) by logit margins of $\Delta z < 0.5$. High-frequency truncation eliminates these critical separating hyperplanes, inducing categorical collapse.
  2. The Permutation Invariance Coordinate Dilemma: In an unconstrained transformer hidden state $h \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ ($D=2048$), individual coordinate indices carry arbitrary order. Applying a 1D Discrete Fourier Transform along unpermuted coordinate indices interprets arbitrary dimension slots as sequential temporal samples, reducing the spectrum to trivial white noise.

2. Graph Laplacian & SVD Fiedler Permutation

To afford 1D harmonic analysis its strongest possible mathematical advantage, coordinates must be optimally reordered such that correlated dimensions are topologically adjacent. Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ denote the residual feature matrix collected across forward passes. We define the dimension affinity graph $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, where $W_{ij} = |\text{Corr}(X_{:,i}, X_{:,j})|$.

The Graph Laplacian matrix is formulated as $L = D_{\text{deg}} - W$. Minimizing the 1D continuous quadratic placement cost $\sum_{i,j} W_{ij} (\pi(i) - \pi(j))^2$ subject to $\|\pi\|_2 = 1$ and $\pi \perp \mathbf{1}$ relaxes directly to the spectral bisection problem:

L v_2 = \lambda_2 v_2, \quad \text{where } \lambda_2 > 0 \text{ is the Algebraic Connectivity (Fiedler Value)}

Under mean-centered and column-normalized activations $\tilde{X}$, the second right singular vector $v_2$ from the thin Singular Value Decomposition $\tilde{X} = U \Sigma V^T$ is analytically identical to the Fiedler vector of the normalized covariance graph. The optimal 1D topological ordering permutation $\pi$ is determined by:

\pi = \text{argsort}(v_2)

Theorem 1 (Permutation Group Isomorphism): Let $P_{\pi} \in \{0, 1\}^{D \times D}$ denote the permutation matrix corresponding to $\pi$. Because $P_{\pi}^T P_{\pi} = I_D$, reordering coordinates preserves the exact $L_2$ norm and geometric inner product structure: $\langle P_{\pi} x, P_{\pi} y \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle$. The transformation is strictly loss-free.

4. Mathematical Proof of the Topological Dimension Barrier

Why does optimal SVD spectral reordering fail to compress deep transformer layers? We formalize the mechanism via the Topological Dimension Barrier Theorem:

\mathcal{M}_{\text{residual}} \subset \mathbb{S}^{D-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^D, \quad \text{dim}(\mathcal{M}) = d_{\text{intrinsic}} \gg 1

Theorem 2 (Boundary Tearing under Dimension Collapse): Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a compact Riemannian manifold with intrinsic topological dimension $d \ge 2$ embedded in $\mathbb{R}^D$. Any bijective or homeomorphic mapping $f: \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^1$ from $\mathcal{M}$ to a 1D continuous interval induces unbounded metric distortion:

\sup_{x, y \in \mathcal{M}} \frac{|f(x) - f(y)|}{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x, y)} \cdot \sup_{x, y \in \mathcal{M}} \frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x, y)}{|f(x) - f(y)|} = +\infty

Proof Sketch: Under the Whitney Embedding Theorem, a smooth $d$-manifold requires at least $2d$ Euclidean dimensions for an embedding without self-intersection or boundary singularities. Mapping a $d$-dimensional hyperspherical manifold ($d \gg 1$) onto a 1D line forces topological space-filling curve behavior (analogous to Hilbert/Peano curves). Adjacent points on the manifold are inevitably torn apart in 1D space, creating sharp artificial step discontinuities. In the frequency domain, step discontinuities generate infinite Dirichlet harmonic tails ($S(k) \propto 1/k$), dispersing spectral energy evenly across all high-frequency bins. This produces the observed invariant white-noise floor ($\gamma \approx 0.58$).

Corollary (Failure of 1D Truncation): Retaining 90% of residual energy under an equiangular white spectrum requires retaining over 90% of all frequency bins ($k_{\text{cutoff}} > 922 / 1024$), offering negligible compression while introducing severe Phase and Argmax distortion. Naive 1D-FFT compression of deep transformers is mathematically void.

5. The High-Dimensional Subspace Steering Alternative

The falsification of 1D frequency-domain truncation does not imply that neural representations are unsteerable. Rather, it reveals that the legitimate operational arena of deep neural networks is high-dimensional subspace projection, not 1D linear wave filtering.

h_{\text{steered}}^{(L)} = h^{(L)} + \alpha \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\perp} v_{\text{carrier}}

In prior work (PULSAR Technote Part 2), we established that intervention vectors $v_{\text{carrier}}$ projected via thin QR factorization into the Null-Space ($\mathbf{P}_{\perp} = I - Q Q^T$) of protected knowledge anchors achieve residual cross-interference of strictly $1.958 \times 10^{-8}$. Coupled with native Layer 16 SwiGLU gating insulation and microsecond closed-loop PID damping, targeted carrier injection in Layer 13 achieves 100% factual steering on occluded tasks while preserving bitwise $L2 = 0.000\text{e}+00$ rollback fidelity upon carrier detachment. Subspace carrier injection operates intrinsically within the 2048-dimensional non-Euclidean manifold, perfectly respecting the Topological Dimension Barrier.
