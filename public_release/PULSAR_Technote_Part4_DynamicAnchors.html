1. Introduction & The Dimensionality Crisis

The quest for lifelong model editing in autoregressive Transformers has long been impeded by the fundamental conflict between edit locality and retention. Landmark locate-then-edit frameworks, notably ROME and MEMIT, demonstrated that factual associations reside predominantly within middle-layer Feed-Forward Networks (FFN). To prevent collateral damage to unrelated knowledge, recent pioneering work such as AlphaEdit introduced null-space projection, enforcing that parameter perturbations $\Delta W$ satisfy:

$\Delta W \cdot K_{\text{preserve}} = \mathbf{0} \quad \implies \quad \Delta W = \Delta W_0 (I - Q Q^T)$

where $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times r}$ forms an orthonormal basis for the preserved key representations.

While mathematically elegant in single-batch scenarios, lifelong sequential editing encounters an insurmountable theoretical bottleneck: the static null-space collapse. Real-world world knowledge comprises tens of thousands of facts ($M \gg 2048$). When $M \ge D$, the empirical key matrix approaches full rank ($\text{rank}(K) \to D$). Consequently, the static null-space projection operator vanishes:

$\lim_{M \to D} \dim(P_{\perp}) = D - \text{rank}(K) = 0$

Under this constraint, attempting to force further factual updates creates severe ill-conditioning, leading to exponential parameter norm-growth ($\|\Delta W\|_F \gg 10^3$), numeric instability, and universal cognitive degradation.

2.1 Dynamic Contextual Subspace Theorem

Our first core insight is that inference is intrinsically local. When generating tokens concerning statutory taxation, the model does not require active geometric separation from orbital mechanics or biochemistry.

Let $\mathcal{K}_{\text{global}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{global}} \times D}$ denote the global repository of protected anchors. For an input hidden state $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$, we define the Active Semantic Cone:

$\mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}(x) = \{ k_i \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{global}} \mid \cos(x, k_i) \ge \tau \}$

Theorem 1 (Local Degree of Freedom Invariance). In natural language manifolds, restricting the active cone cardinality to $r \le 8 \ll 2048$ ensures that the localized null-space operator $P_{\perp, \text{local}} = I - Q_{\text{active}} Q_{\text{active}}^T$ preserves a guaranteed dynamic degree of freedom:

$\dim(P_{\perp, \text{local}}) = D - r = 2048 - 8 = 2040 \quad (\mathbf{99.609\%})$

By deploying a lightweight Thalamic Router, local basis extraction and projection execute within P95 36.71 µs on commodity CPUs, completely bypassing global dimension exhaustion.

2.2 18-Layer Cascaded Manifold Sharding

Academic editing methods consistently commit the error of single-layer cramming, forcing hundreds of disparate updates into Layer 5 or 6. In PULSAR, the residual stream is treated as an 18-stage cascaded processing assembly:

$\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\text{cascaded}}) = \sum_{l=0}^{17} D_l = 18 \times 2048 = \mathbf{36,864} \text{ dimensions}$

Knowledge anchors are stratified hierarchically across layers:

  • Tier 0 (Layers 00~03): Morphological and syntactic invariants;
  • Tier 1 (Layers 04~10): Encyclopedic entities and physical relations;
  • Tier 2 (Layers 11~15): Abstract causal reasoning and mathematical chains;
  • Tier 3 (Layers 16~17): Statutory lexicons and terminal token crystallization.

2.3 SwiGLU Gating & The Common-Mode Carrier Reality

Theoretical literature often assumes that the SwiGLU FFN gating mechanism:

$\text{FFN}(x) = (\text{Swish}(x W_{\text{gate}}) \odot x W_{\text{up}}) W_{\text{down}}$

will naturally yield near-zero activations on non-target domains. However, physical testing demonstrates that in native base models, the strongest activating neurons respond symmetrically (1.00x) across disparate domains (Gate 1 FAIL). Native neurons act as broadband common-mode carriers; external deterministic formal rules are essential for genuine domain enforcement.
