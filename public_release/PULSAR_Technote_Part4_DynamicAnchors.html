1. Introduction & The Falsification Objective

The pursuit of lifelong knowledge editing in autoregressive Transformers has spurred widespread theoretical claims regarding parameter locality and null-space protection. Influential frameworks including ROME, MEMIT, and AlphaEdit posit that factual memories can be isolated within middle-layer Feed-Forward Networks (FFN) and safeguarded via linear projection:

$\Delta W \cdot K_{\text{preserve}} = \mathbf{0} \quad \implies \quad \Delta W = \Delta W_0 (I - Q Q^T)$

However, literature often conflates mathematical formulation with physical silicon reality, presuming that: (a) single-layer residual streams ($D=2048$) can indefinitely absorb updates without full-rank collapse ($M \ge D \implies P_{\perp} \to 0$); (b) FFN gating naturally segregates disparate domains; and (c) FP32 projection leaves mathematically zero residual.

The explicit objective of this technical note is to subject these assumptions to an open, empirical stress-test on unquantized Google Gemma-2B base weights on commodity hardware.

2.1 The Dynamic Contextual Subspace Hypothesis

We hypothesize that inference is intrinsically local. Rather than maintaining global static orthogonality across tens of thousands of encyclopedic facts, single-token inference operates within an Active Semantic Cone:

$\mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}(x) = \{ k_i \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{global}} \mid \cos(x, k_i) \ge \tau \}$

If active cone cardinality is bounded ($r \le 8 \ll 2048$), the dynamic degree of freedom remains preserved:

$\dim(P_{\perp, \text{local}}) = D - r = 2048 - 8 = 2040 \quad (\mathbf{99.609\%})$

We evaluate whether this dynamic projection can execute within a microsecond budget on commodity x86-64 CPUs.

2.2 Cascaded 18-Layer Manifold Sharding

To prevent single-layer rank saturation, knowledge updates are distributed across the 18 cascaded layers totaling $\sum_{l=0}^{17} D_l = 18 \times 2048 = \mathbf{36,864}$ dimensions, organized across morphological, encyclopedic, causal, and terminal token tiers.

2.3 The Spontaneous Domain Gating Hypothesis

Literature frequently conjectures that non-linear SwiGLU gating:

$\text{FFN}(x) = (\text{Swish}(x W_{\text{gate}}) \odot x W_{\text{up}}) W_{\text{down}}$

spontaneously suppresses activations on non-target domains. We empirically test this hypothesis by measuring the activation ratio of peak neurons across disparate linguistic domains.
